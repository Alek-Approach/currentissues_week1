Text A · FIFA press release (adapted)

FIFA's report reveals how the best teams are reshaping football

FIFA has published a comprehensive report on the 2026 World Cup, promising "unprecedented insights" into how the best national teams and players on the planet are changing the modern game. FIFA's own experts studied every one of the 104 matches, combining football knowledge with advanced data and video.

The main finding is that counter-pressing, winning the ball back immediately after losing it, was one of the defining trends of the tournament. Champions Spain were especially adept at turning a lost ball into an instant attack.

The report also shows there was no single route to success. Cabo Verde, for example, proved that compact, well-organised defending can help a team compete with more talented opponents. Goalkeepers, meanwhile, are evolving from shot-stoppers into active playmakers.

Arsène Wenger, FIFA's Chief of Global Football Development, described the World Cup as "the highest level of our game" and said the aim was to understand not just what happened, but why. The report is available to coaches, analysts and other stakeholders on the FIFA Training Centre.

Text B · model independent report (written for class)

FIFA marks its own homework: tactics report silent on off-field debates

FIFA's new review of the 2026 World Cup is detailed, but it is a review of FIFA's tournament written by FIFA's own staff. It studies all 104 matches and highlights trends such as counter-pressing, which helped Spain beat Argentina in the final, and the growing role of goalkeepers.

What the report does not discuss is just as revealing. There is nothing on ticket prices, which fan groups strongly criticized before the tournament, when FIFA used demand-based pricing for the first time. There is nothing on the summer heat that players' unions warned about, or on the costs for travelling fans and host cities.

A related FIFA article praises the new 48-team format as "competitive", yet the press release quotes only FIFA staff: no players' union, no independent researchers and no fans.

To be fair, a technical report is meant to focus on how football is played, not on business decisions. Still, when an organization evaluates its own event, readers should ask who chose the questions, and which questions were left out.

Text A: original B2+ adaptation of FIFA's press release, with short quotations attributed to FIFA. Text B: written for this course to model an independent frame. Its background points (pre-tournament criticism of ticket pricing and heat warnings) were widely reported before the tournament.

Official report · B2+ adaptation

"Sunny, cheerful and peaceful": Munich reviews a record Oktoberfest

P1The City of Munich has summed up this year's Oktoberfest as "sunny, cheerful and peaceful". According to initial estimates, around 7.4 million people visited the festival over its 16 days, up from 6.5 million in 2025, making it the most visited Oktoberfest in history. The report adds that the festival was overshadowed by a fatal accident involving an employee of one of the rides.

P2Organizers credit the late-summer weather and a visitor management campaign called "How to Wiesn", which encouraged people to come on weekdays. Weekday visits rose by about 25%. A new security concept, with a security centre, more cameras, AI technology and staff watching crowd movements, was used to prevent overcrowding. On some busy Saturdays, the main entrances were closed for around 30 minutes and visitors were sent to side entrances.

P3Sales were generally strong. Beer consumption rose 2.7% to around 6.7 million litres, while demand for alcohol-free beer grew by almost 11%. However, ride and stall operators said guests were more restrained in their spending and often simply walked around.

P4Police received around 1,600 calls, more than last year, mostly linked to drug offences. The ambulance service treated 18.5% more patients, although only 1.1% were taken to hospital. On the first day, a security worker was knocked over and injured when crowds rushed into the tents. Officials attribute these increases mainly to the higher number of visitors. Separately, the safety inspectors who check the rides reported "significantly lower" numbers of injuries than in previous years.

P5On the environmental side, electricity use fell by about 7%, and around 92 tonnes of rubbish were collected from the festival grounds. Use of the free drinking-water fountains rose by more than 50%. Around 4,000 lost items were handed in, and stewards collected about 105,000 beer mugs that visitors had tried to take home.

Original adaptation (about 370 words) of the City of Munich's "Preliminary Final Report on Oktoberfest 2026". Short phrases in quotation marks are from the report.
